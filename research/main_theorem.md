# Candidate main results

Status labels: **PROVED** (complete argument below), **CONDITIONAL**, **CONJECTURED**,
**DISPROVED**. Nothing here has been checked by a second person.

## Setting

* A generation produces a pair (Y, C): answer Y in a countable set, duration C > 0 (output
  tokens). Pairs from distinct generations are i.i.d. with an arbitrary joint law P; Y and
  C may be dependent. The mode a* of the law of Y is unique.
* K ≥ 1 workers. A *launch policy* decides, from everything observed so far, when a free
  worker starts a new generation. Launches are numbered 1, 2, … in order of start time
  (ties by worker index). Launch i yields (Y_i, C_i), drawn afresh at its start.
* A *sequential rule* R is a pair (τ, â) where τ is a stopping time for σ(Y_1, …, Y_n) and
  â is a function of Y_1, …, Y_τ. R is δ-correct if P(τ < ∞, â ≠ a*) ≤ δ for every law of Y
  with a unique mode. (PPR, CITE with the target chosen in advance, and — conditionally, see
  the audit — MMC are of this type.)
* *Consumption orders* for feeding R on K workers:
  * **completion order**: the n-th answer given to R is the n-th generation to finish;
  * **launch order**: the n-th answer given to R is Y_n, available once launches 1, …, n
    have all finished.
  In both, all generations still running when R stops are cancelled.

## Proposition 0 — sequential costs do not change the problem. PROVED

For K = 1 and any stopping time τ with E[τ] < ∞: E[Σ_{t≤τ} C_t] = E[C]·E[τ]. Proof and
consequences in `research_directions.md` §0. Hence no cost-aware stopping theorem exists in
the serial model beyond the known sample-count theory.

## Theorem 1 — launch-order consumption preserves validity. PROVED

*Let R be δ-correct. For every K, every launch policy, and every joint law of (Y, C), R fed
in launch order satisfies P(τ < ∞, â ≠ a*) ≤ δ.*

*Proof.* Let G_i be the σ-field generated by the launch times of launches 1, …, i and by
(Y_j, C_j), j < i. The decision to make the i-th launch, and its start time, depend only on
events observed before that start, which are functions of earlier launches' pairs and of
the policy; the pair (Y_i, C_i) is generated afresh at the start. Hence (Y_i, C_i) is
independent of G_i and has law P, so (Y_i)_{i≥1} is an i.i.d. sequence with the law of Y.
R is applied to this sequence and its stopping index and decision are functions of
Y_1, …, Y_τ alone; the physical delay before Y_n becomes available changes when R acts, not
what it computes. The i.i.d. guarantee applies verbatim. ∎

The same argument gives validity for any consumption order fixed in advance on the
per-worker streams (for example round-robin over workers).

## Proposition 2 — completion-order consumption does not. PROVED

*Suppose R, on an i.i.d. stream, stops and returns x whenever its first m answers all equal
x (m = 4 for Adaptive-Consistency at 0.95; m = 6 for MMC with the Beta(½, ½) mixture at
ε = 0.1; m = 3 for ConSol's default). Fix any answer A with P(Y = A) = q > 0, not the mode.
Let C = 1 when Y = A and C = r ≥ m otherwise, and let every free worker relaunch at once.
Then, fed in completion order,*

  P(R stops and returns A) ≥ P(NB(K, 1 − q) ≥ m),

*where NB(K, 1 − q) is the number of successes (probability q) before the K-th failure. The
right-hand side tends to 1 as K → ∞, for every q > 0.*

*Proof.* No generation with Y ≠ A finishes before time r. Before its first non-A draw,
worker k finishes G_k generations with answer A, at times 1, …, G_k, where G_k is geometric
with P(G_k ≥ g) = q^g, independently across workers. If Σ_k G_k ≥ m then at least m
A-generations finish by time m ≤ r: either some G_k ≥ m, or all G_k < m and all Σ G_k of them
finish by time m. In that event the first m completed answers are all A and R returns A.
Σ_k G_k has the stated negative-binomial law. ∎

Numerically (analytic bound; simulation in `race_counterexample.py` agrees): with q = 0.3,
K = 16: 0.87 for m = 4 and 0.64 for m = 6. With q = 0.1, K = 64: 0.91 and 0.70. A 10%
answer is "certified" at ε = 0.1 two times in three.

Remarks.
* The failure is not specific to unanimity rules; the bias of the completed set is at most
  K answers, so any rule that can stop on evidence of the order of K votes is exposed, and
  rules needing evidence ≫ K are nearly unaffected. CONJECTURED quantitative form: for a
  rule whose i.i.d. error is δ and whose evidence threshold is a margin of M votes, the
  completion-order error is at most the i.i.d. error of the same rule with margin M − (K−1).
  Not proved.
* The length ratio need not be extreme: r = 2 already gives 0.32 for MMC with 16 workers
  (simulation).
* Classical antecedent to check before claiming novelty: Glynn & Heidelberger on the bias
  of time-budgeted parallel replications.

## Open problem 3 — the price of validity. CONJECTURED / OPEN

Launch order is valid but wasteful: finished answers wait behind a slow earlier launch, and
all work launched after the stopping index is discarded. With all workers always busy the
tokens spent are K·T, where T is the wall time at which launches 1, …, τ have all finished.

Target statement (not proved): *among launch policies and consumption orders that are valid
for every joint law of (Y, C), characterise the minimum of E[tokens] subject to a wall-time
constraint, and show that a windowed launch-order policy (never more than W launches beyond
the consumed prefix) is optimal up to a constant.* Ingredients that would be needed:

1. an upper bound for windowed launch order: waste ≤ (number of outstanding launches) ×
   their residual durations; with window W this is at most W − 1 generations, giving
   E[tokens] ≤ E[C]·(E[τ] + W − 1) and wall time ≈ E[C]·E[τ]/min(K, W) plus a
   head-of-line term involving E[max of W durations]. **Not yet written out.**
2. a lower bound showing that no distribution-free valid procedure can consume an answer
   ahead of an earlier, unfinished launch of the same worker stream without knowing the law
   of C given Y. **This is the hardest lemma and has not been attempted.** It may be false
   as stated: validity might be recoverable by discarding a bounded, data-independent set of
   answers. Deciding this is the next mathematical task.
3. if the law of C given Y is known or estimated, a compensated wall-clock e-process that
   uses every finished answer. Valid in principle; needs the hazard of each answer class.

## Status

Proposition 0, Theorem 1 and Proposition 2 are complete and elementary. The efficiency
question (problem 3) is open, and the novelty of Theorem 1 and Proposition 2 depends on
a full reading of the related work listed in `novelty_matrix.md`.
